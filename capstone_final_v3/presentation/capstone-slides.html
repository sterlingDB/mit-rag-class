Design, evaluation, and engineering trade-offs

THE PROBLEM

Example research questions

One fact

“What overall pick was Jeremiyah Love in the 2026 NFL draft?”

Retrieve the selection and its supporting passage.

A comparison across drafts

“what spot did Jeremiyah Love get drafted and compare it to the same round of 5 older drafts for Arizona.”

Resolve the round and draft years, then support every comparison.

A connection across articles

“What is the capital of the state that the actor who played Cooter Davenport in The Dukes of Hazzard represented in Congress?”

Support the actor, represented state, and capital.

A date and an amount

“When and for how much did the Brady Bunch house sell?”

Support both the sale date and the price.

Existing evaluation prompts. The Arizona comparison does not specify which five older drafts.

THE SOLUTION

System workflow

  1. Load and indexExtract local HTML text, embed articles, preserve source IDs.
  2. RetrieveCombine BM25 keyword matches with vector similarity.
  3. Choose a pathValidate a cache hit, answer directly, or search and follow links.
  4. Answer with sourcesGenerate from bounded excerpts and cite article IDs.

Article links connect indexed local pages. The agent does not browse missing pages.

ENGINEERING DECISIONS

Retrieval choices
and context limits

ChoiceTrade-off
Equal-weight keyword and vector scoresCombines matching signals; weights need evaluation.
Local article-link traversalAdds related evidence within the saved collection.
3 action rounds, 8,000 context charactersBounds work but can stop before finding enough evidence.
Whole articles, leading 2,000-character excerptsSimple implementation can omit the relevant passage.

SAFETY AND COST

Each extra check
has a cost

  • Input cleaning and XML escaping protect prompt boundaries.
  • Cached answers still require validation.
  • Filtering, routing, and quality checks add model calls.
  • Chat cost estimates exclude embedding costs.

Savings require equivalent answer quality. Defenses do not prove general attack resistance.

LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
